Diagram Chasing in Abelian Categories

Reading notes on subobjects, exactness, and the snake lemma.

Toni Annala's Diagram Chasing in Abelian Categories (original PDF) develops diagram chasing by following subobjects through maps. These reading notes explain the setup, isolate the image and inverse-image calculation, and work through a connecting homomorphism in abelian groups.

What to track

In an abelian category a subobject of XX is represented by a monomorphism into XX, with two representatives identified if they are isomorphic over XX. When checking exactness at the middle object of A→uB→vCA \xrightarrow{u} B \xrightarrow{v} C, compare the subobjects im⁡(u)\operatorname{im}(u) and ker⁡(v)\ker(v) of BB. The equation vu=0vu=0 gives one inclusion. The reverse inclusion is the step that has to be proved.

For f:X→Yf:X\to Y, send a subobject S⊆XS\subseteq X to the image of S→X→YS\to X\to Y. For T⊆YT\subseteq Y, its inverse image f−1(T)f^{-1}(T) is the pullback of T→YT\to Y along ff. Factoring through a monomorphism and applying the pullback property give

f(S)⊆T⟺S⊆f−1(T)f(S)\subseteq T \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\subseteq f^{-1}(T)

A useful image calculation

The identity f(f−1(T))=T∩im⁡(f)f(f^{-1}(T))=T\cap\operatorname{im}(f) explains exactly how much of a target subobject can be reached. Write I=im⁡(f)I=\operatorname{im}(f) and factor ff as an epimorphism X↠IX\twoheadrightarrow I followed by the inclusion I↪YI\hookrightarrow Y. Then f−1(T)f^{-1}(T) is also the pullback of X→IX\to I along I∩T↪II\cap T\hookrightarrow I. The projection onto I∩TI\cap T is an epimorphism because pullbacks preserve epimorphisms in an abelian category. Its composite with I∩T↪YI\cap T\hookrightarrow Y therefore has image I∩TI\cap T.

In ℤ\mathbb Z-modules, take f(n)=6nf(n)=6n and T=4ℤT=4\mathbb Z. The inverse image is 2ℤ2\mathbb Z, and its image is 12ℤ12\mathbb Z, the intersection 4ℤ∩6ℤ4\mathbb Z\cap6\mathbb Z.

A snake-lemma calculation

Consider the following diagram in abelian groups. Both rows are the usual short exact sequence for reduction modulo 33.

0⟶ℤ→×3ℤ→qℤ/3⟶0↓×3↓×3↓00⟶ℤ→×3ℤ→qℤ/3⟶0\begin{array}{ccccccccc} 0 & \longrightarrow & \mathbb Z & \xrightarrow{\times 3} & \mathbb Z & \xrightarrow{q} & \mathbb Z/3 & \longrightarrow & 0\\ && \downarrow\scriptstyle{\times3} && \downarrow\scriptstyle{\times3} && \downarrow\scriptstyle{0} \\ 0 & \longrightarrow & \mathbb Z & \xrightarrow{\times 3} & \mathbb Z & \xrightarrow{q} & \mathbb Z/3 & \longrightarrow & 0 \end{array}

The connecting map goes from the kernel of the right vertical map to the cokernel of the left one. Both are ℤ/3\mathbb Z/3. Lift a class [n][n] to nn in the top middle group. Moving down gives 3n3n, whose preimage under the bottom horizontal injection is nn. Taking its class in the left cokernel gives [n][n] again. Replacing nn by n+3kn+3k changes that preimage by 3k3k, so the class is independent of the chosen lift. With this convention the connecting map is the identity.

Reading onward

  • Read the original note's first two sections for the conventions on quotients and subobjects.
  • For the image and inverse-image section, keep track of which ambient object contains each subobject before comparing them.
  • When reading the exact-sequence arguments, mark the squares whose pullback properties give a lift, and the cokernels that make the resulting class independent of choices.
  • For a reference on these constructions, see The Stacks Project, Abelian categories and its Snake lemma.
