Conventions, points and corrections to the source

For the finite-type comparison fix a separated Noetherian scheme S. The objects are separated S-schemes of finite type. The flat-chain definitions also make sense on the big category of schemes over S, after the usual size choice; on the finite-type category use the generating families that lie there. The fpqc comparison with the canonical topology follows from descent on the big site, applied to those families. “Finer” means containing more covering sieves, not necessarily strictly more.

A sieve on X is a collection of arrows into X closed under precomposition. A Grothendieck topology specifies which sieves cover, with the maximal sieve covering and with covering preserved under pullback and transitivity. A generating family stands for the sieve it generates. A join is the smallest topology containing all its listed generators. For a fixed underlying category, finer topologies impose more sheaf conditions.

In the point descriptions, an affine S-scheme P is local for a topology if every map P → X lifts to one member of each covering family of X. The associated fibre functor uses the filtered colimit over finite-type factorizations, as in Gabber–Kelly, Theorems 2.3 and 2.6. These test objects concern the big finite-type site; small-site points, such as the geometric points of the small étale site, are a different assertion. An absolutely integrally closed domain is integrally closed in an algebraically closed fraction field.

The passports correct the Zar join, the finite symbol, the cdf point and the nontrivial-extension condition for ℓdh\ell\mathrm{dh}. The étale and smooth nodes represent the same topology. A missing arrow does not establish incomparability.

On an affine scheme Spec⁡A\operatorname{Spec}A the closed subsets are V(I)={𝔭:I⊆𝔭}V(I)=\{\mathfrak p:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}. Every scheme is sober: a nonempty irreducible closed subset has a unique generic point. An affine open meeting that subset meets it densely and gives a generic point by the prime ideal description. Two distinct points have distinct closures, since an affine open containing one and hence all of its generizations reduces the assertion to the affine case. For a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme, the constructible (patch) topology generated by quasi-compact opens and their complements is compact Hausdorff.
