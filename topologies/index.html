Conventions, points and corrections to the source

For the finite-type comparison fix a separated Noetherian scheme S. The objects are separated S-schemes of finite type. The flat-chain definitions also make sense on the big category of schemes over S, after the usual size choice; on the finite-type category use the generating families that lie there. The fpqc comparison with the canonical topology follows from descent on the big site, applied to those families. “Finer” means containing more covering sieves, not necessarily strictly more.

A sieve on X is a collection of arrows into X closed under precomposition. A Grothendieck topology specifies which sieves cover, with the maximal sieve covering and with covering preserved under pullback and transitivity. A generating family stands for the sieve it generates. A join is the smallest topology containing all its listed generators. For a fixed underlying category, finer topologies impose more sheaf conditions.

In the point descriptions, an affine S-scheme P is local for a topology if every map P → X lifts to one member of each covering family of X. The associated fibre functor uses the filtered colimit over finite-type factorizations, as in Gabber–Kelly, Theorems 2.3 and 2.6. These test objects concern the big finite-type site; small-site points, such as the geometric points of the small étale site, are a different assertion. An absolutely integrally closed domain is integrally closed in an algebraically closed fraction field.

The graph retains all original labels as node IDs and all 28 relations. The passports correct the Zar join, the finite symbol, the cdf point and the nontrivial-extension condition for ℓdh. The étale and smooth nodes represent the same topology; the atlas treats equality in both directions. The figure is a comparison of the listed relations, not a claim of incomparability for every unjoined pair.

Relative properties concern a specified morphism. Absolute properties concern one scheme; some are determined by its underlying space and others use the structure sheaf. Local and global refer to the way the condition is tested.

Stacks 01WG · finite and integral morphisms

Underlying spaces

On an affine scheme Spec A the closed subsets are V(I) = {𝔭 : I ⊆ 𝔭}. The closure of a point 𝔭 is V(𝔭), so specialization corresponds to inclusion of prime ideals. The principal opens D(f) are quasi-compact, form a basis and satisfy D(f) ∩ D(g) = D(fg).

Every scheme is sober: a nonempty irreducible closed subset has a unique generic point. An affine open meeting that subset meets it densely and gives a generic point by the prime ideal description. Two distinct points have distinct closures, since an affine open containing one and hence all of its generizations reduces the assertion to the affine case. For a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme, the constructible (patch) topology generated by quasi-compact opens and their complements is compact Hausdorff. A subset closed in the patch topology is Zariski closed exactly when it is stable under specialization.

Same topological space, different scheme

Spec k and Spec k[ε]/(ε²) each have one point; only the first is reduced. The reduction map is a universal homeomorphism.

Separated is not Hausdorff

The scheme A¹ₖ is separated over k. Its generic point lies in every nonempty open, so the Zariski space is not Hausdorff.

Quasi-affine need not be affine

The punctured affine plane U = A²ₖ ∖ {(0,0)} is quasi-affine. Its global ring is k[x,y], by intersecting k[x,y]ₓ and k[x,y]ᵧ in k(x,y). The canonical map to its global spectrum omits the origin and is not an isomorphism.

Quasi-separated need not be separated

Glue two copies of A¹ₖ along D(t) by the identity. Their intersection is affine, and the finite affine cover makes the scheme quasi-separated. On the product of the two charts over k, the diagonal is the affine diagonal with the origin removed, so it is not closed.

Connected need not be irreducible

Spec k[x,y]/(xy) is the union of two closed affine lines meeting at the origin. Each line is connected, hence their union is connected.

The local ring at the origin of Spec k[t²,t³] is a domain but does not contain the element t, which is integral over it. Thus the cusp is reduced and not normal.
