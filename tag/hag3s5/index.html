Definition 3.33 · System of homogeneous polynomial equations#

Let kk be a ring and let II and JJ be sets. A system of JJ homogeneous polynomial equations in II variables over kk is a JJ-tuple Σ=(fj)j∈J\Sigma =(f_{j})_{j\in J} of homogeneous polynomials in k[xi∣i∈I].k[x_{i}\vert i\in I]. We denote by (Σ) the homogeneous ideal in k[xi∣i∈I]k[x_{i}\vert i\in I] generated by (fj)j∈J(f_{j})_{j\in J} and by k[Σ]k[\Sigma ] the ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded kk-algebra k[xi∣i∈I]/(Σ).k[x_{i}\vert i\in I]/(\Sigma ).

The point of considering homogeneous polynomial equations is that they have well-defined solutions in projective space, as we now explain.

Definition 3.38 · Projective scheme#

A functor CAlg Set is a projective kk-scheme if it is isomorphic to hSol for some system of homogeneous polynomial equations Σ in finitely many variables over k.k. We denote by Proj Fun(CAlg Set) the full subcategory spanned by the projective kk-schemes.

Remark 3.39#

The finiteness condition in the definition of projective kk-scheme is a matter of convenience. It implies for example that any projective kk-scheme is locally of finite type (see Definition 2.81). We will later see that projective kk-schemes are proper, and for this the finiteness condition is essential.

Next, we want to show that the solution functor hSol depends only on the ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded kk-algebra k[Σ],k[\Sigma ], analogously to Sol Spec(k[Σ])k[\Sigma ]) (Lemma 2.22). We first note that not every ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded kk-algebra AA is isomorphic to one of the form k[Σ]:k[\Sigma ]: this is the case if and only if AA is generated by A1A_{1} as a kk-algebra, i.e., if and only if the canonical map Sym is surjective. The following construction will be generalized to arbitrary ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded algebras in §3.7, but the case of algebras generated in degree 1 is by far the most important case in practice.
