## Definitions and notation {#sec:equivalence}

::: definition def:equivalence | Equivalence relation
An **equivalence relation** on a set $X$ is a reflexive, symmetric, and transitive relation $\sim$. The equivalence class of $x \in X$ is

$$
[x] = \{y \in X : y \sim x\} \label{eq:equivalence-class}
$$
:::

A **partition** of $X$ is a collection of pairwise disjoint, nonempty subsets whose union is $X$. The empty collection partitions the empty set.

## The partition into classes

::: proposition prop:partition | Equivalence relations and partitions
The distinct classes of an equivalence relation partition $X$. Conversely, the relation of belonging to the same member of a partition is an equivalence relation, and its classes recover the partition.
:::

::: proof pf:partition | Equivalence relations and partitions
By reflexivity and \(\eqref{eq:equivalence-class}\), every $x$ belongs to $[x]$. Thus each class is nonempty and the classes cover $X$. If $z \in [x]\cap[y]$, symmetry and transitivity give $x\sim y$. A second application of transitivity gives both inclusions $[x]\subseteq[y]$ and $[y]\subseteq[x]$.

Conversely, each element belongs to exactly one member of the partition. The relation of sharing a member is reflexive and symmetric. If $x,y$ share a member and $y,z$ share a member, uniqueness for $y$ forces those two members to coincide. This proves transitivity and identifies the classes.
:::

::: example ex:fibers
[The fibres of a linear map](ref:prop:kernel-cosets) are the classes of the relation of having the same image.
:::
