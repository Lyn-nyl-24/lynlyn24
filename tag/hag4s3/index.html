Warning 4.26#

Different closed subfunctors can have the same open complement, since different ideals can have the same radical (e.g., (x)(x) and (x2)(x^{2}) in ℤ[x]).\mathbb{Z}[x]). For this reason, there is no notion of “closed complement” of an open subfunctor.

Remark 4.28 · Loci associated with maps of quasi-coherent modules#

The loci from Definition 3.44 have a direct generalization to any algebraic functor X:X: a map f:M→Nf:M\rightarrow N in Mod defines subfunctors V(f),f), Epi(f),f), Mono(f),f), and Iso(f)f) of X.X. These are characterized by the property that their preimage along any RR-point x:x: Spec(R)→XR)\rightarrow X is the corresponding locus for the RR-linear map f(x):M(x)→N(x).f(x):M(x)\rightarrow N(x). Proposition 3.47 then immediately generalizes to this setting: if NN is a vector bundle, then V(f)f) is a closed subfunctor and Epi(f)f) an open subfunctor of X,X, and if both MM and NN are vector bundles, then also Mono(f)f) and Iso(f)f) are open subfunctors of X.X.

Call a subfunctor clopen if it is both closed and open. Recall that Idem(R)R) is the set of idempotent elements in a ring R.R.
