Sieves and descent

Section 6.1 · Sheaves

In this chapter, we introduce the formalism of Grothendieck topologies and sheaves. A Grothendieck topology τ\tau on a category 𝒞\mathcal{C} determines (and is determined by) a subcategory Sh of the presheaf category P(𝒞),\mathcal{C}), whose objects are called τ\tau-sheaves on 𝒞.\mathcal{C}. These are presheaves on 𝒞\mathcal{C} satsfying a certain local-to-global condition with respect to the topology τ,\tau , called τ\tau-descent. In algebraic geometry, we typically use this formalism in the following situations:

(i) 𝒞\mathcal{C} is the category of open subsets of a topological space T,T, which has a canonical Grothendieck topology; one then speaks of sheaves on T.T. This was the original notion of sheaf discovered by Leray in the 1940s, before Grothendieck’s categorical generalization in the 1950s.

(ii) 𝒞=\mathcal{C}= CAlg is the opposite of the category of kk-algebras, or equivalently the category Aff of affine kk-schemes, on which there is a plethora of commonly used Grothendieck topologies. The main ones are the Zariski, Nisnevich, étale, fppf (“fidèlement plat de présentation finie”), and fpqc (“fidèlement plat quasi-compact”) topologies.

(iii) The topologies in (ii) extend to various enlargements of Aff such as the category Sch of kk-schemes (to be introduced in §7) and even the whole category Fun(CAlg Set) of algebraic kk-functors. All the “Zariski descent” statements established so far are examples of descent statements for the so-called Zariski topology on CAlg (e.g., Corollary 2.72 and Proposition 3.57) or on Fun(CAlg Set) (e.g., Corollary 4.44).
