## A morphism of exact sequences {#sec:snake-diagram}

Work in the category of abelian groups. The rows below are short exact, and the squares commute.

~~~figure exact-rows
~~~

::: theorem thm:snake | Snake lemma for short exact rows
There is an exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\ker f'\longrightarrow\ker f\longrightarrow\ker f''
\xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{coker}f'
\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}f\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}f''
\longrightarrow 0
$$
:::

~~~figure snake-sequence
~~~

::: proof pf:snake | Snake lemma for short exact rows
For $x\in\ker f''$, choose $a\in A$ with $p_A(a)=x$. Commutativity gives $p_B(f(a))=0$, so there is a unique $b'\in B'$ with $i_B(b')=f(a)$. Define $\delta(x)=[b']$. A different lift of $x$ differs by $i_A(a')$ and changes $b'$ by $f'(a')$. This proves well-definedness, and taking sums of lifts proves additivity.

If $\delta(x)=0$, write $b'=f'(a')$. Then $a-i_A(a')$ is in $\ker f$ and still maps to $x$. Conversely, lifting $x$ by an element of $\ker f$ gives $\delta(x)=0$. This proves exactness at $\ker f''$.

A class $[b']$ maps to zero in $\operatorname{coker}f$ exactly when $i_B(b')=f(a)$ for some $a$. In that case $p_A(a)\in\ker f''$ and $\delta(p_A(a))=[b']$. For exactness at $\operatorname{coker}f$, if $p_B(b)=f''(a'')$, lift $a''$ to $a$. Then $b-f(a)$ is in the image of $i_B$. Exactness at $\ker f$ follows by lifting along $i_A$, and the two endpoint assertions follow from injectivity of $i_A$ and surjectivity of $p_B$.
:::
