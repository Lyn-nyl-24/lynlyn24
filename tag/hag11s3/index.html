Remark 11.31#

The finiteness condition in the definition of projectivity serves three independent purposes: it implies firstly that projective morphisms are locally of finite type (if AA is an ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded kk-algebra of finite type, then any A(f)A_{(f)} is a kk-algebra of finite type), and secondly that they are quasi-compact (Example 7.36). Remark 11.32 · Elimination theory#

The fact that ℙRn→\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R}\rightarrow Spec(R)R) is a closed map is concretely the following statement, which is classically known as the main theorem of elimination theory. For any system of homogeneous polynomial equations Σ in finitely many variables over R,R, there exists a unique

radical ideal I⊂RI\subset R with the following property: for any RR-field k,k, there exists a solution of Σ in some algebraic closure of kk if and only if II goes to zero in k.k. In other words, we can “eliminate all variables” in Σ, which leaves us with a system of polynomial equations in 0 variables. The radical ideal II is called the resultant of the system Σ.

The converse of Proposition 11.27 does not hold. In fact, over any field, there are examples of proper schemes that are not projective, although they are not easy to write down. The following theorem shows that proper schemes are nevertheless close to being projective, and more precisely can always be obtained from projective schemes by “blowing down”. This is a very useful result that is often used to reduce statements about proper morphisms to projective morphisms.

Theorem 11.33 · Chow’s lemma#

Let SS be qcqs scheme, XX a separated SS-scheme of finite type, and U⊂XU\subset X an open subscheme such that UU is quasi-projective over SS (e.g., any affine open subscheme). Then there exists a finitely presented closed subscheme Z⊂XZ\subset X with Z∩U=∅Z\cap U=\varnothing such that Bl is quasi-projective over S.S\textit{.} Hence, if XX is proper over S,S\textit{,} then Bl is projective over S.S\textit{.}

Remark 11.37#

It follows from Zariski’s main theorem (Remark 9.105) that the conditions (i)–(iii) of Corollary 11.36 are further equivalent to:

(iv) ff is quasi-finite and proper.

(v) ff is locally quasi-finite, universally closed, and separated.
