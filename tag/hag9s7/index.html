Bertini's theorem

Section 9.7 · Smoothness

Bertini’s theorem states that a generic hyperplane section of a smooth quasi-projective scheme over a field is again smooth. The proof uses a fundamental idea in geometry, which is to promote a set of interest to a geometric object. In this case, the set of hyperplanes in a projective space over kk can be identified with the set of kk-points of the dual projective space. We then estimate

the Krull dimension of the closure of the set of “bad” hyperplanes (those for which the conclusion of the theorem fails) in this dual projective space to deduce that the open complement is nonempty.

Definition 9.110 · Hyperplane, hyperplane section#

Let kk be a ring and let VV be a kk-module.

(i) A hyperplane in ℙ(V)\mathbb{P}(V) is a closed subscheme of the form ℙ(W),\mathbb{P}(W), where WW is a quotient module of VV such that ker(V↠W)V\twoheadrightarrow W) is a line.

(ii) Given a subscheme X⊂ℙ(V),X\subset \mathbb{P}(V), a hyperplane section of XX is a closed subscheme of XX of the form X∩H,X\cap H, where H⊂ℙ(V)H\subset \mathbb{P}(V) is a hyperplane.
