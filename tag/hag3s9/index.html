Definition 3.86 · Closure of subfunctor#

Let XX be an algebraic functor and Y⊂XY\subset X a subfunctor. The closure of YY in XX is the smallest closed subfunctor of XX containing Y.Y. This is well-defined as arbitrary intersections of closed subfunctors are closed (by Proposition 2.60(i)).

Definition 3.91 · Finite scheme#

Let kk be a ring. An algebraic kk-functor XX is a finite kk-scheme if there exists a finite kk-algebra AA such that X≃X\simeq Spec(A).A).

Remark 3.93#

Conversely, if an affine kk-scheme is projective, then it is finite; we will prove this later using the notion of properness (Corollary 11.36). This means that any closed subscheme of 𝔸kn,\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}, if not finite, must “go to infinity” and not remain closed in ℙkn.\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}. This is in stark contrast to the analogous situation in differential geometry, where there exist positive-dimensional closed submanifolds of ℝn\mathbb{R}^{n} that are “away from infinity”, i.e., remain closed in ℙn(ℝ).\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{R}).
