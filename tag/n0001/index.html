Equivalence relations and partitions

Recover a partition from an equivalence relation, and conversely.

  • Definitions and notation
  • The partition into classes

Definitions and notation #

Definition 1.1.1 · Equivalence relation#

An equivalence relation on a set XX is a reflexive, symmetric, and transitive relation ∼\sim. The equivalence class of x∈Xx \in X is

[x]={y∈X:y∼x}[x] = \{y \in X : y \sim x\}
(1.1.1)

A partition of XX is a collection of pairwise disjoint, nonempty subsets whose union is XX. The empty collection partitions the empty set.

The partition into classes

Proposition 1.1.2 · Equivalence relations and partitions#

The distinct classes of an equivalence relation partition XX. Conversely, the relation of belonging to the same member of a partition is an equivalence relation, and its classes recover the partition.

Proof · Equivalence relations and partitions#

By reflexivity and (1.1.1)\text{(1.1.1)}, every xx belongs to [x][x]. Thus each class is nonempty and the classes cover XX. If z∈[x]∩[y]z \in [x]\cap[y], symmetry and transitivity give x∼yx\sim y. A second application of transitivity gives both inclusions [x]⊆[y][x]\subseteq[y] and [y]⊆[x][y]\subseteq[x].

Conversely, each element belongs to exactly one member of the partition. The relation of sharing a member is reflexive and symmetric. If x,yx,y share a member and y,zy,z share a member, uniqueness for yy forces those two members to coincide. This proves transitivity and identifies the classes.

Example 1.1.3#

The fibres of a linear map are the classes of the relation of having the same image.
