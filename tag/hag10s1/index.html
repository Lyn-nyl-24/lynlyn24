Section 10.1 · Divisors and blowups

Blowing up is a ubiquitous construction in algebraic geometry, which serves many purposes, such as eliminating indeterminacies of rational maps and resolving singularities. As another example, consider the affine nodal cubic N=N= V(y2−x3−x2)⊂𝔸2.y^{2}- x^{3}- x^{2})\subset \mathbb{A}^{2}. At the singular point (0,0),(0,0), there are two tangent directions to N.N. In both of these examples, the key feature of the blowup construction is that the preimage of the blown-up point is an effective Cartier divisor, i.e., a closed subscheme locally given by the vanishing locus of a single function that is not a zero divisor.
