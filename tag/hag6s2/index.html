Grothendieck topologies and sheaves

Section 6.2 · Sheaves

Definition 6.13 · Grothendieck topology, site#

A Grothendieck topology τ\tau on a category 𝒞,\mathcal{C}, or topology for short, assigns to each object X∈𝒞X\in \mathcal{C} a collection of sieves on X,X, called τ\tau-covering sieves, subject to the following conditions:

(i) If RR is a τ\tau-covering sieve on XX and f:Y→Xf:Y\to X is any map, then the pullback f∗(R)f^{\ast}(R) is a τ\tau-covering sieve on Y.Y.

(ii) Let SS be a sieve on XX and let RR be a τ\tau-covering sieve on X.X. If for all f:Y→Xf:Y\to X in R,f∗(S)R,f^{\ast}(S) is τ\tau-covering, then SS itself is τ\tau-covering.

(iii) The maximal sieve on any XX is τ\tau-covering. A family of maps (Yi→X)i∈I(Y_{i}\to X)_{i\in I} is called a τ\tau-covering family or a τ\tau-cover if it generates a τ\tau-covering sieve. A category equipped with a Grothendieck topology is called a site.

Remark 6.14#

The following further properties of a Grothendieck topology τ\tau are consequences of the axioms (i)–(iii):

(iv) If RR is a τ\tau-covering sieve on XX and SS is any sieve on XX containing R,R, then SS is τ\tau-covering.

(v) Any finite intersection of τ\tau-covering sieves on XX is τ\tau-covering.

Definition 6.15 · Sheaf#

Let 𝒞\mathcal{C} be a category and τ\tau a topology on 𝒞.\mathcal{C}. A presheaf F∈F\in P(𝒞)\mathcal{C}) is called a τ\tau-sheaf if it satisfies descent along every τ\tau-covering sieve. We denote by Sh P(𝒞)\mathcal{C}) the full subcategory of τ\tau-sheaves.

Remark 6.16 · Poset of topologies#

Let τ\tau and ρ\rho be topologies on 𝒞.\mathcal{C}. We say that τ\tau is coarser than ρ\rho and that ρ\rho is finer than τ,\tau , and we write τ≤ρ,\tau \le \rho , if every τ\tau-covering sieve is a ρ\rho-covering sieve. The collection of topologies on 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a poset under the relation ≤.\le . By definition, any intersection of topologies on 𝒞\mathcal{C} is again a topology. The poset of topologies on 𝒞\mathcal{C} therefore admits all infima, and hence also all suprema. In particular, any collection σ\sigma of sieves on objects of 𝒞\mathcal{C} generates a topology which is the coarsest topology on 𝒞\mathcal{C} containing σ.\sigma .

Example 6.17#

On any category 𝒞,\mathcal{C}, we can consider the following topologies:

(i) The discrete topology is the finest topology: all sieves are covering. The final presheaf ∗\ast is the only sheaf in the discrete topology (by Example 6.6).

(ii) The indiscrete topology is the coarsest topology: only the maximal sieves are covering. All presheaves are sheaves in the indiscrete topology.

Example 6.19 · Weiss topologies#

Let TT be a topological space and κ\kappa a cardinal. The κ\kappa-Weiss topology on Open(T)T) is defined as follows: a sieve RR on UU is covering if any subset of UU of size <κ<\kappa is contained in some element of R.R. This recovers the discrete topology for κ=0\kappa =0 and the indiscrete topology for (Example 6.17). For κ=1,\kappa =1, the covering sieves are exactly the nonempty sieves, and the corresponding sheaves are the constant presheaves. For κ=2,\kappa =2, the covering sieves are those of the canonical topology (Example 6.18), except that the empty sieve does not cover the empty set.

Warning 6.20#

The notion of Grothendieck topology is not a direct generalization of the notion of topology on a set. Example 6.18 explains the relation between the two notions. Historically, Grothendieck named the new concept “topology” because it replaced topological spaces in the context of sheaf theory.

Definition 6.24 · Pretopology#

A pretopology π\pi on a category 𝒞\mathcal{C} assigns to each object X∈𝒞X\in \mathcal{C} a collection of families of 𝒞/X,\mathcal{C}_{/X}, called π\pi-covering families or π\pi-covers, subject to the following conditions:

(i) If (Ui→X)i∈I(U_{i}\to X)_{i\in I} is a π\pi-covering family and f:Y→Xf:Y\to X is any map, then the pullbacks Ui×XYU_{i}\times _{X}Y exist in 𝒞\mathcal{C} and the family (Ui×XY→Y)i∈I(U_{i}\times _{X}Y\to Y)_{i\in I} is π\pi-covering.

(iii) The singleton family (id is π\pi-covering.

The topology associated with a pretopology π\pi is the coarsest topology for which π\pi-covering families generate covering sieves (which exists by Remark 6.16).

Example 6.25#

Let τ\tau be a topology on 𝒞.\mathcal{C}. If (and only if) 𝒞\mathcal{C} has pullbacks, then the τ\tau-covering families form a pretopology on 𝒞,\mathcal{C}, whose associated topology is τ.\tau . For example, if XX is a topological space or an algebraic functor, covering families for the canonical topology on Open(X)X) are precisely open coverings, which in particular form a pretopology on Open(X).X).

Example 6.27 · The standard topology on Top#

Open coverings form a pretopology on the category Top of all topological spaces (and many similar categories, like that of smooth manifolds). If τ\tau is the associated topology, Proposition 6.26 implies the following:

(i) A sieve on XX is τ\tau-covering if and only if it contains an open covering of X.X. Hence, a family (Yi→X)i∈I(Y_{i}\to X)_{i\in I} is τ\tau-covering if and only if it locally has sections, i.e., if and only if every point of XX has a neighborhood that maps to some YiY_{i} over X.X.

(ii) A presheaf F:F: Top Set is a τ\tau-sheaf if and only if is a sheaf on TT for each T∈T\in Top.

For example, for every X∈X\in Top, the presheaf of XX-valued functions is a τ\tau-sheaf (by Example 6.18).

Remark 6.30 · Finitary topologies#

Let τ\tau be a topology on 𝒞\mathcal{C} associated with a pretopology whose covering families are finite. By Proposition 6.26, the condition of being a τ\tau-sheaf can then be expressed using only finite limits. It follows that the subcategory Sh P(𝒞)\mathcal{C}) is closed under filtered colimits. This applies to the Zariski topology on CAlg and all the topologies from Example 6.29.

Example 6.34 · Basis of a topological space#

Let TT be a topological space and let ℬ⊂\mathcal{B}\subset Open(T)T) be a basis of its topology. Example 6.35 · Sheaves on manifolds#

Let Man be the category of nn-dimensional topological manifolds, equipped with the standard topology (induced by the pretopology of open coverings). Since by definition every nn-manifold admits an open covering by manifolds homeomorphic to ℝn,\mathbb{R}^{n}, the full subcategory {ℝn}⊂\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\subset Man spanned by ℝn\mathbb{R}^{n} is dense. Remark 6.38#

The comparison lemma remains true for sheaves of categories (as in Remark 6.12). Applied to 𝒞=\mathcal{C}= CAlg and τ\tau the Zariski topology, Example 6.37 shows that a Zariski sheaf of categories on CAlg extends uniquely to a sheaf of categories on Fun(CAlg Set). By the proof of the comparison lemma, this extension is given by right Kan extension, i.e., by forming categories of quasi-coherent objects (Definition 4.13). Granted this, Example 6.37 justifies Remark 4.43.
