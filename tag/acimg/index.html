The image and inverse image functors

Images and inverse images of subobjects along a morphism.

Definition 2.3.1#

Let f:X→Yf : X \to Y be a map. We define the image functor associated to ff from the category of subobjects of XX to the category of subobjects of YY by sending each subobject UU to the image of U→X→YU \to X \to Y . If we have a map of subobjects U→U′U \to U^{\prime }, then fU′f U^{\prime } is a subobject trough which U→X→YU \to X \to Y factors, so we have a map fU→fU′f U \to f U^{\prime }. Functorality is satisfied by the uniqueness of maps of subobjects.

Using the image functor, we obtain yet another characterization for monicness of a map.

Proposition 2.3.2#

A map f:X→Yf : X \to Y is monomorphism if and only if the image functor associated to ff is injective.

Definition 2.3.6#

Let f:X→Yf : X \to Y be a map. We define the inverse image functor, f−1f^{-1}, from the category of subobjects of YY to the category of subobjects of XX by simply setting f−1U=U×YXf^{-1}U=U\times_Y X. By the properties of fibre product U×YX→XU \times _{Y} X \to X will always be monic. The induced mappings are just the ones given by the fibre product.

Corollary 2.3.10#

Let f:X→Yf : X \to Y be epic. Now ff−1U=Uff^{-1}U=U.

Proof#

By previous, it is enough to show that f−1ff−1U=f−1Uf^{-1}ff^{-1}U=f^{-1}U. We know that ff−1Uff^{-1}U is contained in UU. The image of f−1Uf^{-1}U is contained in ff−1Uff^{-1}U, and if VV is a strictly larger subobject of XX, then fVf V could not be contained in UU, and therefore it would not be contained in ff−1Uff^{-1}U either. This will be very useful in diagram chasing, if a subobject is contained in the image of some map, then the image of its preimage is the subobject itself! Moreover, using the previous theorem, we can say something about the image of an intersection.
