Definition 4.40 · Zariski-local epimorphism#

A map of algebraic functors Y→XY\to X is called a Zariski-local epimorphism if for every Spec(R)→X,R)\to X, there exists f1,...,fn∈Rf_{1},...,f_{n}\in R generating the unit ideal such that each composite Spec( Spec(R)→XR)\to X lifts to Y.Y.

Example 4.41#

(i) Any epimorphism is a Zariski-local epimorphism. In particular, any map with a section, such as the projection 𝔸n×X→X,\mathbb{A}^{n}\times X\to X, is a Zariski-local epimorphism.

(ii) Let (Fi)i∈I(F_{i})_{i\in I} be a family of subsets of 𝒪(X)\mathcal{O}(X) whose union generates the unit ideal. Then the map D(Fi)→XF_{i})\to X is a Zariski-local epimorphism.

(iii) Let LL be a line bundle over X.X. A family (si)i∈I(s_{i})_{i\in I} of global sections of LL is called generating if, for every x∈x\in El(X),X), the family (si(x))i∈I(s_{i}(x))_{i\in I} generates the line L(x).L(x). In this case, the map D(si)→Xs_{i})\to X is a Zariski-local epimorphism.

(iv) The map 𝔸I−0→ℙI\mathbb{A}^{I}- 0\to \mathbb{P}^{I} is a Zariski-local epimorphism, by Proposition 3.24. More generally, for any ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded ring A,A, the map D(A1)→A_{1})\to Proj is a Zariski-local epimorphism (Remark 3.63).
