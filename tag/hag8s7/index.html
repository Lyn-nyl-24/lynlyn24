Section 8.7 · Properties of schemes

Let RR be a ring and AA an RR-algebra. Recall that an element a∈Aa\in A is integral over RR if it satisfies a monic polynomial equation with coefficients in R,R, or equivalent if the RR-subalgebra of AA generated by aa is finite over R.R. The set of all such elements is an RR-subalgebra of A,A, called the integral closure of RR in A.A. An integral domain RR is integrally closed if it equals its integral closure in its fraction field Frac(R).R).

Example 8.85#

By Gauss’s lemma on primitive polynomials, unique factorization domains are integrally closed. In particular, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum theorem (Theorem 8.81(ii)), regular local rings are integrally closed domains.

Example 8.95 · Dedekind schemes#

Let XX be a locally noetherian scheme of Krull dimension ≤1.\le 1. By Corollary 8.94(i), XX is normal if and only if it is regular. Such a scheme XX is called a Dedekind scheme. An affine scheme Spec(R)R) is a Dedekind scheme if and only if RR is a finite product of Dedekind domains (by Remark 8.90). If XX is any locally noetherian integral scheme of Krull dimension ≤1,\le 1, the Krull–Akizuki theorem implies that the normalization XνX^{\nu} is again locally noetherian, and is therefore a Dedekind scheme.
