Section 7.1 · Schemes

Definition 7.1 · Scheme#

A scheme is a functor X:X: CAlg →\rightarrow Set satisfying the following two conditions:

(i) XX is a sheaf for the Zariski topology on CAlg (Example 6.28);

(ii) XX admits an open covering by affine schemes (Definition 5.40).

We denote by Sch ⊂\subset Sh(CAlg the full subcategory spanned by schemes. For any scheme S,S, we denote by Sch the slice category Sch whose objects are called SS-schemes. When S=S= Spec(k)k) for some ring k,k, an SS-scheme is also called a kk-scheme.

Our goal in this section is to investigate the closure properties of the category of schemes inside the larger category of Zariski sheaves.

Example 7.3 · Examples of schemes#

(i) For any ring A,A, the affine scheme Spec(A)A) is a scheme, since it is a Zariski sheaf by Corollary 2.72. By Proposition 7.2, every quasi-affine scheme is also a scheme.

(ii) For any ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded ring A,A, Proj(A)A) is a scheme: it is a Zariski sheaf by Proposition 3.57, and it admits an open covering by the affine schemes D(f)≃f)\simeq Spec(A(f))A_{(f)}) for f∈A+f\in A_{+} homogeneous (Example 5.42(iii)). In particular, for any ring k,k, every projective kk-scheme is a scheme. By Proposition 7.2, every quasi-projective kk-scheme is also a scheme.

(iii) The affine line with doubled origin (Example 4.48) is a scheme: it admits an open covering by two copies of 𝔸1,\mathbb{A}^{1}, and it follows from Zariski descent for modules that it is a Zariski sheaf.

(iv) We have the following relative generalization of (i) and (ii): if XX is a scheme and Y→XY\rightarrow X is either a quasi-affine morphism (Definition 4.34) or a quasi-projective morphism (Definition 4.35), then YY is also a scheme.

(v) Let kk be a ring, MM a kk-module, and n∈ℕ.n\in \mathbb{N}. Proposition 7.2 and the Plücker embedding from Example 3.100 show that the Grassmannian Gr is a scheme. More generally, for any increasing sequence of natural numbers n=(n1,...,ns),n=(n_{1},...,n_{s}), the flag scheme Flag is a scheme, being a closed subfunctor of the projective space by Examples 3.103, 3.100, and 3.95.

Remark 7.22 · Zariski topology on the category of schemes#

By Proposition 7.2, any open subfunctor of a scheme is a scheme. Hence, open coverings form a pretopology on Sch, whose associated topology is called the Zariski topology. Definition 7.23 · Schematic morphism#

A map of algebraic functors Y→XY\rightarrow X is called schematic if, for every map S→XS\rightarrow X where SS is a scheme, the pullback Y×XSY\times _{X}S is a scheme.

Example 7.24#

(i) Any morphism between schemes is schematic, by Theorem 7.10(i).

(ii) Closed immersions, open immersions, and immersions are schematic, by Proposition 7.2.

(iii) Affine, quasi-affine, projective, and quasi-projective morphisms (Definitions 4.34 and 4.35) are schematic. This follows from the stability of these properties under base change (which in turn follows from the compatibility of relative Spec and Proj with base change) and Example 7.3(iv).
