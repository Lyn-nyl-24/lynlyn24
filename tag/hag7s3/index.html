Quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness

Section 7.3 · Schemes

By Proposition 7.17, any scheme can be written as the colimit of a diagram of affine schemes and open immersions. A scheme is called quasi-compact and quasi-separated, or qcqs for short, if a finite such diagram exists. In this section, we discuss in more detail this finiteness condition, which makes sense more generally for algebraic functors.

Definition 7.30 · Quasi-compact and quasi-separated#

(i) An algebraic functor XX is called quasi-compact if, for every Zariski-local epimorphism X,X, there exists a finite subset J⊂IJ\subset I such that is a Zariski-local epimorphism.

(ii) A morphism of algebraic functors f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X is called quasi-compact if, for every morphism Z→XZ\rightarrow X with ZZ quasi-compact, the pullback Y×XZY\times _{X}Z is quasi-compact.

(iii) An algebraic functor XX is called quasi-separated if, for every diagram Y→X←ZY\rightarrow X\leftarrow Z with YY and ZZ quasi-compact, the pullback Y×XZY\times _{X}Z is quasi-compact.

(iv) A morphism of algebraic functors f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X is called quasi-separated if its diagonal Δf:Y→\Delta _{f}:Y\rightarrow Y×XYY\times _{X}Y is quasi-compact.

Proposition 7.31 · Characterizations of quasi-compactness#

For an algebraic functor X,X\textit{,} the following conditions are equivalent:

(i) XX is quasi-compact;

(ii) the unique map X→X\rightarrow Spec(ℤ)\mathbb{Z}) is quasi-compact;

(iii) there exists a Zariski-local epimorphism where II is finite and each YiY_{i} affine.

Proposition 7.32 · Characterizations of quasi-separatedness#

For an algebraic functor X,X\textit{,} the following conditions are equivalent:

(i) XX is quasi-separated;

(ii) the unique map X→X\rightarrow Spec(ℤ)\mathbb{Z}) is quasi-separated;

(iii) for all maps Y→XY\rightarrow X and Z→XZ\rightarrow X where YY and ZZ are affine, Y×XZY\times _{X}Z is quasi-compact.

Proposition 7.33 · Composition of qc/qs morphisms#

Consider a commutative triangle of algebraic functors

(i) If ff and gg are quasi-compact, then hh is quasi-compact.

(ii) If ff and gg are quasi-separated, then hh is quasi-separated.

(iii) If hh is quasi-compact and ff is quasi-separated, then gg is quasi-compact.

(iv) If hh is quasi-separated, then gg is quasi-separated.

(v) If hh is quasi-compact and gg is a Zariski-local epimorphism, then ff is quasi-compact.

(vi) If hh is quasi-separated and gg is quasi-compact and a Zariski-local epimorphism, then ff is quasi-separated.

Example 7.35 · Quasi-compact immersions#

(i) By Proposition 7.31(iii), a closed subfunctor of a quasi-compact algebraic functor is again quasi-compact. It follows that any closed immersion is quasi-compact. In particular, as the terminology suggests, any separated morphism is quasi-separated.

(ii) Let RR be a ring and let I⊂R.I\subset R. Considering the open covering (D(f)⊂f)\subset D(I))f∈II))_{f\in I} of D(I),I), we see that the scheme D(I)I) is quasi-compact if and only if the radical ideal is finitely generated (i.e., equals for some finite set J).J). Hence, an open immersion is quasi-compact if and only if the corresponding quasi-coherent radical ideal is finitely generated.

(iii) Any monomorphism is quasi-separated, since its diagonal is an isomorphism.

Example 7.36 · Quasi-affine and quasi-projective schemes#

(i) By Example 7.35(ii), any quasi-affine scheme is quasi-compact. It is also quasi-separated, since it is separated by Example 7.29(ii).

(ii) Let AA be an ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded ring and let I⊂AI\subset A be a homogeneous subset. As in Example 7.35(ii), D(I)I) is quasi-compact if and only if the saturated radical ideal generated by II is finitely generated. In particular, for any ring k,k, any quasi-projective kk-scheme is quasi-compact. It is also quasi-separated, since it is separated by Example 7.29(iii).

(iii) It follows from (i,ii) and Proposition 7.34 that any quasi-affine or quasi-projective morphism of algebraic functors is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.

Remark 7.40#

By Propositions 7.37 and 7.38, a scheme is quasi-compact and quasi-separated (often abbreviated as qcqs) if and only if it can be written as a finite colimit of affine schemes as in Proposition 7.17. This finiteness property propagates to many invariants: for example, the underlying space of a qcqs scheme is spectral, and its category of quasi-coherent modules is compactly generated. As Example 7.36 shows, most schemes of interest are qcqs. Examples of schemes that are not quasi-compact are infinite coproducts of nonempty schemes and projective spaces ℙkI\mathbb{P}^{I}_{k} where II is an infinite set and kk a nonzero ring. An infinite-dimensional affine space with doubled origin is an example of a quasi-compact scheme that is not quasi-separated.

The following standard definition combines the local “algebraic” finiteness properties of §2.9 with the global “topological” finiteness properties of this section:

Definition 7.41 · Morphism of finite presentation/type#

Let f:X→Sf:X\rightarrow S be a map of algebraic functors.

(i) ff is of finite presentation if it is locally of finite presentation, quasi-compact, and quasi-separated.

(ii) ff is of finite type if it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact.

For example, any quasi-projective morphism is of finite type.
