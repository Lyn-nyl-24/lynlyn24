Over the complex numbers, the nodal cubic N(ℂ)⊂ℂ2N(\mathbb{C})\subset \mathbb{C}^{2} looks like a punctured pinched torus, i.e., a torus in which one of the circles bounding a hole is collapsed to a point; this is equivalently a punctured sphere in which two points have been identified. The cuspidal cubic C(ℂ)⊂ℂ2C(\mathbb{C})\subset \mathbb{C}^{2} looks like a punctured sphere with a single thorn. The two ways of visualizing N(ℂ)N(\mathbb{C}) correspond to two ways to algebraically “resolve” the nodal singularity of NN at the origin:

(i) The “pinched torus” picture suggests viewing NN as a degenerate member of a family of curves NλN_{\lambda} given (for example) by the equations y2=x(x+λ)(x+1).y^{2}=x(x+\lambda )(x+1). While an arbitrary system of polynomial equations does not in general have a nonsingular deformation as in (i), it is a deep theorem that, if we work over a field of characteristic zero, it is always possible to resolve singularities by blowing up as in (ii). Whether this is always possible in positive characteristic is a major open question in algebraic geometry.
