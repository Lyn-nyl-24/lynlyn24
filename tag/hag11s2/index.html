Section 11.2 · Properness

To express the existence and uniqueness of limits in algebraic geometry, we need to specify a class of inclusions such that “every point of XX may be approached from X′X^{\prime} in one and only one way”. On the one hand, we want X′X^{\prime} to be “dense” in X,X, in the sense that functions on XX should be determined by their restrictions to X′.X^{\prime}. Given a closed subscheme Z⊂XZ\subset X disjoint from X′,X^{\prime}, the directions of approach to ZZ in XX are classifed by the projectivized normal cone ℙ\mathbb{P}(C which is the preimage of ZZ in the blowup Bl (Corollary 10.36). Thus, one may encode the other half of the desired condition by requiring the blowup Bl to be an isomorphism for any ZZ disjoint from X′.X^{\prime}. By the universal property of blowups (Proposition 10.24), this is equivalent to requiring any such ZZ to be an effective divisor. This motivates the following more precise definition:

(ii) A Prüfer domain is an integral domain RR such that (Spec(R),R), Spec(Frac(R)))R))) is a Prüfer pair.

(iii) A Prüfer scheme is a scheme that admits an affine open covering by Prüfer domains.

Remark 11.9 · Affine Prüfer pairs#

Let R→R′R\rightarrow R^{\prime} be a localization. Then (Spec(R),R), Spec(R′))R^{\prime})) is a Prüfer pair if and only if R→R′R\rightarrow R^{\prime} is injective and every finitely generated ideal I⊂RI\subset R with IR′=R′IR^{\prime}=R^{\prime} is a line. In particular, a domain is Prüfer if and only if every nonzero finitely generated ideal is a line. It follows that the locally noetherian Prüfer schemes are exactly the Dedekind schemes (Example 8.95).

Remark 11.10 · Prüfer implies normal#

Prüfer domains are integrally closed, and hence Prüfer schemes are normal. Indeed, consider any fraction r/sr/s over a Prüfer domain R.R. By definition, the ideal (r,s)(r,s) is then a line. Hence, there are elements f1,...,fn∈Rf_{1},...,f_{n}\in R generating the unit ideal such that the fraction r/sr/s simplifies over RfiR_{fi} to a fraction ri/sir_{i}/s_{i} with (ri,si)=Rfi.(r_{i},s_{i})=R_{fi}. Let RR be a domain with fraction field K.K\textit{.} The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) RR is a local Prüfer domain.

(ii) The divisibility relation on RR is total.

(iii) The poset of ideals in RR is totally ordered.

(iv) The poset of divisors Div(Spec(R))R)) is totally ordered.

Definition 11.13 · Valuation ring#

A valuation ring is an integral domain satisfying the equivalent conditions of Proposition 11.12.

Remark 11.14 · Value group#

If RR is a valuation ring with fraction field K,K, there is an initial choice of valuation v:K→Γ∪{∞}v:K\rightarrow \Gamma \cup \{\infty \} with R=𝒪v,R=\mathcal{O}_{v}, namely the divisor map div :K×↠K×/R×=:K^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow K^{\times}/R^{\times}= Div(Spec(R)).R)). The totally ordered abelian group of divisors Div(Spec(R))R)) is therefore also called the value group of the valuation ring R.R.

Example 11.15#

(i) Fields are valuation rings, and they are precisely the valuation rings with trivial value group.

(ii) A valuation ring RR is called discrete if its value group is isomorphic to ℤ.\mathbb{Z}. Since ℕ⊂ℤ\mathbb{N}\subset \mathbb{Z} is well-ordered, this implies that RR is a principal ideal domain. Conversely, it is clear that a local principal ideal domain that is not a field is a discrete valuation ring.

(iii) Let Γ be any totally ordered cancellative commutative monoid and let kk be a ring. The ring of Hahn series k[[tΓ]]k[[t^{\Gamma}]] consists of formal sums with aγ∈ka_{\gamma}\in k such that is a well-ordered subset of Γ (this ensures that the product is well-defined). Taking the infimum of this well-ordered set defines a surjective map v:k[[tΓ]]↠Γ∪{∞}.v:k[[t^{\Gamma}]]\twoheadrightarrow \Gamma \cup \{\infty \}. If kk is a field and Γ is a group, then k[[tΓ]]k[[t^{\Gamma}]] is a field and vv is a valuation. Hence, the subring k[[tΓ≥0]]⊂k[[tΓ]]k[[t^{\Gamma \geq 0}]]\subset k[[t^{\Gamma}]] is a valuation ring with value group Γ. When Γ=ℚ,\Gamma =\mathbb{Q}, Hahn series are also called Puiseux series.

Remark 11.16 · Noetherian valuation rings#

Since finitely generated ideals in a valuation ring are principal, noetherian valuation rings are principal ideal domains, hence are either fields or discrete valuation rings. By Example 8.75, these are precisely the regular local rings of Krull dimension ≤1.\leq 1.

Definition 11.18 · Formally prim, formally separated, formally proper#

Let TT be the collection of maps Spec( Spec(V),V), where VV is a valuation ring with fraction field K.K. A morphism of algebraic functors is called:

(i) formally prim if it is a TT -epimorphism;

(ii) formally separated if it is a TT -monomorphism;

(iii) formally proper if it is a TT -isomorphism.

Remark 11.26#

The conclusion of Proposition 11.24 propagates to separated and proper morphisms, by applying it to the diagonal. Note that the inclusion C′⊂CC^{\prime}\subset C in the statement is an example of a Prüfer pair (Definition 11.8). For separated and proper morphisms, the uniqueness part of the lifting property allows us to generalize it even further, analogously to the case of unramified and étale morphisms (Remark 9.98): one can show that separated and proper morphisms satisfy their respective lifting property with respect to arbitrary Prüfer pairs.
