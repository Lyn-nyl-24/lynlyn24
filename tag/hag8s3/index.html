For a ring the following conditions are equivalent:

(i) RR is noetherian.

(ii) Every submodule of a finitely generated RR-module is finitely generated.

(iii) Mod is an abelian category such that the inclusion Mod Mod is exact.

(iv) Every RR-module of finite type is of finite presentation: Mod Mod

(v) Every RR-algebra of finite type is of finite presentation: CAlg CAlg

Remark 8.25#

Condition (v) in Proposition 8.24 implies that, if RR is noetherian, then any finitely generated RR-algebra is again noetherian. The fact that the polynomial ring R[x1,...,xn]R[x_{1},...,x_{n}] is noetherian is Hilbert’s basis theorem.

Definition 8.26 · Locally noetherian, noetherian scheme#

A scheme XX is locally noetherian if there is an affine open covering (Ui⊂X)i∈I(U_{i}\subset X)_{i\in I} such that each ring 𝒪(Ui)\mathcal{O}(U_{i}) is noetherian. A scheme is noetherian if it is locally noetherian and quasi-compact.

Remark 8.30 · Coherent rings#

A ring RR is called coherent if Mod is an abelian category such that the inclusion Mod Mod is exact. By Proposition 8.24, noetherian rings are coherent, but the converse fails. For example, any valuation ring is coherent, and if RR is noetherian and II is any set, then the polynomial ring is coherent. One can show that “coherent” is also a local property of rings, so that it extends uniquely to a local property of schemes.

Proposition 8.31#

A scheme XX is noetherian if and only if every descending sequence of closed sub-schemes Z0⊃Z1⊃Z2⊃⋅⋅⋅Z_{0}\supset Z_{1}\supset Z_{2}\supset \cdot \cdot \cdot in XX stabilizes.

Definition 8.32 · Noetherian space#

A topological space TT is noetherian if every descending sequence of closed subsets Z0⊃Z1⊃Z2⊃⋅⋅⋅Z_{0}\supset Z_{1}\supset Z_{2}\supset \cdot \cdot \cdot in TT stabilizes.

Remark 8.33#

If TT is a noetherian space, then TT is quasi-compact. Moreover, any subspace of TT is again noetherian, and hence quasi-compact.

Remark 8.34#

By Proposition 8.31 and Corollary 8.19, if XX is a noetherian scheme, then the underlying space is a noetherian space. However, the converse is not true. Remark 8.35 · Noetherian induction#

To show that all closed subsets of a noetherian space have a certain property P,P, it suffices to show that ZZ has property PP whenever all have property P.P. The same holds for closed subschemes of a noetherian scheme (by Proposition 8.31). This method of proof is called noetherian induction.
