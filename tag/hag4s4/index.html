Remark 4.33 · Morphisms into Spec and Proj#

Using Corollary 2.11, we obtain the following descriptions of maps into Spec(A)A) or Proj(A).A). Let XX and YY be algebraic functors.

(i) For any A∈A\in CAlg a map Y→Y\rightarrow Spec(A)A) consists of a map f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X and a map f∗(A)→𝒪Yf^{\ast}(A)\rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{Y} in CAlg (cf. Remark 2.54).

(ii) Let AA be a quasi-coherent ℕ\mathbb{N}-graded algebra over XX generated by A1.A_{1}. Then a map Y→Y\rightarrow Proj(A)A) consists of a map f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X and a quotient line bundle f∗(A1)↠Lf^{\ast}(A_{1})\twoheadrightarrow L in Mod such that the induced map Sym(f∗(A1))↠f^{\ast}(A_{1}))\twoheadrightarrow Sym(L)L) factors through f∗(A).f^{\ast}(A).

The relative Spec and Proj functors allow us to relativize the notions of affine, quasi-affine, projective, and quasi-projective schemes:

Definition 4.34 · Affine and quasi-affine morphisms#

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X be a map of algebraic functors.

(i) We say that ff is affine, or that YY is affine over X,X, if there exists A∈A\in CAlg and an isomorphism Y≃Y\simeq Spec(A)A) over X.X. We denote by Aff the category of algebraic functors affine over X.X.

(ii) We say that ff is quasi-affine, or that YY is quasi-affine over X,X, if there exists V∈V\in Aff Rad and an isomorphism Y≃Y\simeq D(I)I) over X.X. We denote by QAff the category of algebraic functors quasi-affine over X.X.

Definition 4.35 · Projective and quasi-projective morphisms#

Let f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X be a map of algebraic functors.

(i) We say that ff is projective, or that YY is projective over X,X, if there exists A∈A\in CAlg generated by A1A_{1} and finitely generated, and an isomorphism Y≃Y\simeq Proj(A)A) over X.X. We denote by Proj the category of algebraic functors projective over X.X.

(ii) We say that ff is quasi-projective, or that YY is quasi-projective over X,X, if there exists V∈V\in Proj I∈I\in Rad and an isomorphism Y≃Y\simeq D(I)I) over X.X. We denote by QProj the category of algebraic functors quasi-projective over X.X.

Affineness turns out to be a “quasi-coherent property” in the sense that the functor Aff is right Kan extended from affine schemes:

Warning 4.37#

The analogue of Proposition 4.36(i) does not hold for quasi-affine, projective, and quasi-projective morphisms.

Example 4.38 · Closed immersions as affine morphisms#

By Proposition 4.36, any closed immersion is affine, and Z≃Z\simeq Spec(i∗(𝒪Z)).i_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})). Moreover, an affine map f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X is a closed immersion if and only if the induced map 𝒪X→f∗(𝒪Y)\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow f_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}) is surjective.

Example 4.39 · Integral and finite morphisms#

A map of algebraic functors f:Y→Xf:Y\rightarrow X is integral (resp. finite) if it is affine and the corresponding quasi-coherent algebra A∈A\in CAlg is integral (resp. finite) in the sense of Remark 4.18(ix). Globalizing Proposition 3.92 shows that, if ff is integral, there is a canonical closed immersion over X.X. In particular, finite morphisms are projective.
