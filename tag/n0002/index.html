2.1 Linear maps and kernels

Describe the fibres of a linear map as cosets of its kernel.

  • Setup
  • Fibres
  • Factorisation through the quotient

Setup #

Let VV and WW be vector spaces over the same field and let T ⁣:V→WT \colon V \to W be linear.

Definition 2.1.1 · Kernel of a linear map#

The kernel of TT is the subspace

ker⁡T={v∈V:T(v)=0}.\ker T = \{v \in V : T(v) = 0\}.
(2.1.1)

Fibres

Proposition 2.1.2 · Kernel cosets#

For v∈Vv \in V, the fibre T−1(T(v))T^{-1}(T(v)) is v+ker⁡Tv+\ker T.

Proof

Linearity and (2.1.1)\text{(2.1.1)} give

T(u)=T(v)  ⟺  T(u−v)=0  ⟺  u−v∈ker⁡T.\begin{aligned} T(u) = T(v) &\iff T(u-v)=0 \\ &\iff u-v\in\ker T. \end{aligned}

The last condition is equivalent to u∈v+ker⁡Tu\in v+\ker T. The fibres form a partition as in the equivalence-relation proposition.

Factorisation through the quotient

Theorem 2.1.3 · First isomorphism theorem#

The assignment [v]↦T(v)[v]\mapsto T(v) induces a linear isomorphism V/ker⁡T→im⁡TV/\ker T \to \image T.

Proof

The kernel-coset proposition proves well-definedness and injectivity. Linearity descends from TT, and surjectivity follows from the definition of im⁡T\image T.
